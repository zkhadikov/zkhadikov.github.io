Signature case study

From bachelor prototype to the commercial Jedox GPU Accelerator.

This story shows how an interactive OLAP performance problem moved from bachelor work into product engineering, public research, and durable product documentation.

Timeline

How the GPU case study progressed

  1. 2008–2009

    Bachelor prototype

    A sparse cube representation and CUDA aggregation pipeline cut a 1,176-query view from 30 seconds to 6.2 seconds—approximately 4.8× faster.

  2. 2009

    Public validation

    CeBIT and NVIDIA GTC shared results up to 18× faster for individual queries and over 4× for full views with more than 1,100 queries.

  3. 2009–2012

    Product engineering

    The commercial Jedox GPU Accelerator moved into OLAP server engineering with C/C++, CUDA, multi-GPU scaling, and NVIDIA Tesla support.

  4. Current documentation

    Evidence that stayed public

    Current documentation reports typical 5–10× speedups, best cases around 70×, and workload-dependent limitations.

Problem

Interactive OLAP workloads needed decision-speed responses.

Interactive OLAP aggregation and planning workloads combine sparse multidimensional data, repeated consolidations, and rule evaluation. The goal was to keep demanding finance and planning views responsive instead of turning every recalculation into a long wait.

Insight

Parallel aggregation turned thesis work into public proof.

During 2008–2009, the bachelor prototype used a sparse cube representation and CUDA-based aggregation pipeline for a 1,176-query view. Palo needed 30 seconds, while the prototype completed in 6.2 seconds—approximately 4.8× faster.

Public validation followed in 2009 at CeBIT and NVIDIA GTC. Individual queries were reported at up to 18× faster, and full views with more than 1,100 queries ran at over 4×, moving the work beyond a lab-only result.

  • Palo+GPU: A CUDA-powered In-memory OLAP Server — NVIDIA GTC poster.
  • Exploring Graphics Processing Units as Parallel Coprocessors for Online Aggregation — ACM DOLAP publication.

Engineering

Commercialization required durable systems engineering.

Across 2009–2012, the work became product engineering: C/C++, CUDA, sparse cube representation, massively parallel aggregation, multi-GPU scaling, and integration into the Jedox OLAP server. The public Jedox GPU Accelerator documentation describes the resulting product as an OLAP Server feature that uses NVIDIA Tesla GPUs, supports per-cube activation, and applies dynamic engine selection based on workload fit.

My public Palo server fork and Palo deployment repository relate to open-source server and operations work. They do not contain the proprietary accelerator implementation.

Outcome

Public evidence keeps the benchmark story grounded.

Current official documentation and FAQ show that the commercial Jedox GPU Accelerator was a real product rather than only a research prototype. The public materials describe NVIDIA Tesla GPU support inside Jedox OLAP workflows and explain how workload shape determines whether GPU acceleration is a good fit. The Jedox GPU Accelerator FAQ is the right public source for that current documentation guidance.

Separate research work reported up to 50× faster aggregation and over 150× faster writeback in selected workloads. Those research figures should not be merged with current product documentation guidance.

How the benchmark figures differ

Prototype, research, historical commercial claims, and current product guidance describe different workloads and generations. They should not be interpreted as one universal speedup.

How to read the public source trail

The public Palo repositories document related open-source server and deployment work. They support the broader product context, but they remain separate from the proprietary Jedox GPU Accelerator implementation.
